# Licensing map

`LICENSE` contains the full GNU Affero General Public License v3 text for the public messaging subsystem. Package-specific declarations apply to their source; this workspace is not a blanket relicensing of every directory.

- `packages/crypto`, `packages/session`, `packages/agent`, `apps/agent-browser`, `apps/api`, `packages/contract`, `packages/headless` and `apps/messaging-reference`: AGPL-3.0-only. The broader public messaging scope includes the real relay, persistence/migrations, adapters, trusted controls, reference UI and required build/install inputs and covered dependency sources.
- Newly authored `packages/protocol` and `packages/client`: MIT, with their own complete LICENSE texts.
- Original `apps/web` remains AGPL-3.0-only. Its source derives from the original AGPL frontend; switching its imports to the MIT SDK does not erase that provenance. Its embedded protocol documentation and existing assets retain their applicable grants.
- Independently authored first-party source and documentation in `apps/desk-client` are MIT under its accompanying full `LICENSE`. This grant covers that concrete client, not copied AGPL UI or the separately running messaging implementation. Its recorded authoring inputs were the frozen MIT SDK and public-interface instructions. See the [concrete frontend assessment](docs/release/FRONTEND_BOUNDARY_ASSESSMENT.md).
- The first-party public interface document `docs/architecture/BROWSER_CONSUMER_V1.md` and independent static host `scripts/serve-sdk-consumer.mjs`/its test, `scripts/run-sdk-consumer.mjs` and the ordinary static metadata/download helper `scripts/source-disclosure.mjs`/its test are MIT under `packages/client/LICENSE`, as marked. Other release/build/audit/test orchestration remains covered by the root AGPL unless explicitly marked.
- Third-party sources retain their licenses, copyright and notices. Signal's pinned libsignal and SPQR dependency are AGPL; no MIT notice here overrides them.

First-party authority was confirmed by the owner on October 24, 2026. Previously granted rights remain valid; third-party rights are not claimed or overridden. Different origins, styles, executables, HTTP and an MIT wrapper do not determine copyleft scope. The frontend assessment records provenance, actual imports/build inputs and communication semantics for these clients. A later private integration requires its own concrete assessment if it copies covered source or changes that relationship.

The current [ten-step plan](docs/release/AGPL_MVP_TEN_STEP_PLAN.md) uses documented internal ownership/source/boundary decisions; an external-review certificate is not required. Historical candidate-2 review records remain frozen and do not establish the new release's approval. Step 7 records expanded source/build acceptance. Step 8 implements exact source/notice delivery; final release assessment and owner approval of exact publication manifests remain steps 9–10. Passing engineering tests alone does not close them or authorize publication.
